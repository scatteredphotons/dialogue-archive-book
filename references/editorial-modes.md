# Editorial modes

Read this file only when proposing or writing a chapter.

## Select the voice from the subject

### Technical chronicle

Use for engineering debugging, model training, data design, evaluation, and implementation decisions.

Build the narrative around this movement:

1. What was supposed to work.
2. What actually happened.
3. Which explanations or designs were tried.
4. What evidence overturned them.
5. What solution or narrowed understanding remained.
6. Which lesson transfers to future work.

Keep exact shapes, parameters, errors, and data flow when they matter. Prefer a few decisive snippets or diagrams over transcript-sized code dumps. A little dry humor is welcome, but the technical reasoning remains the protagonist.

### Psychological and intellectual growth

Use for changes in self-understanding, academic agency, exhaustion, ambition, disappointment, and decisions about continuing or letting go.

Write with restrained literary movement and concrete scenes. Do not turn the subject into motivational advice or diagnose the user. Preserve ambiguity where the experience was ambiguous. The important outcome may be a new boundary or a decision rather than success.

### Human observation

Use for laboratory characters, collaboration patterns, internships, and social episodes.

Prefer observable behavior, chronology, and consequences. Humor and a gossip-column rhythm are allowed when the user wants them, but distinguish observation from inferred motive. Do not collapse distinct people into one character or move an anecdote between people for narrative convenience.

## Shared writing constraints

- Each chapter must stand alone for a future reader who cannot see the chat.
- Preserve first-person ownership when the material is the user's experience.
- Give the chapter a memorable title rather than a generic report heading.
- Match length to significance; do not inflate a small episode.
- Avoid AI-flavored summaries, repetitive conclusions, and invented quotations.
- Do not add later knowledge to an earlier scene unless clearly labeled as hindsight.
